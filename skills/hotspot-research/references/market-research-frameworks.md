# Market Research Frameworks

Use this reference only when the report needs structured market, competition, or scenario analysis. Keep the final report grounded in source evidence; frameworks organize evidence, they do not replace it.

## Time-Depth And Current-Map Analysis

Combine two lenses:

- **Time-depth lens**: trace origin, milestones, constraints, path dependency, and phase changes.
- **Current-map lens**: compare present players, substitutes, adoption paths, market structure, and user choices.

Use the intersection to explain why a topic became hot now, what is durable, and what may fade after the news cycle.

## TAM / SAM / SOM

Use for market sizing when credible numeric sources exist.

| Term | Meaning | How To Use |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | Total Addressable Market | The full theoretical market if the solution captured all relevant demand |
| SAM | Serviceable Available Market | The segment reachable under geography, product, regulation, and channel constraints |
| SOM | Serviceable Obtainable Market | The realistic share under competitive and execution constraints |

Rules:

- Cite source, year, geography, methodology, and whether the number is revenue, spend, units, or users.
- Prefer ranges when sources disagree.
- Do not calculate a precise SOM without explicit assumptions.

## PESTLE

Use PESTLE to scan external forces:

| Factor | Questions |
|---|---|
| Political | Subsidies, industrial policy, geopolitical constraints, procurement |
| Economic | Rates, capex cycle, pricing pressure, demand elasticity |
| Social | Public sentiment, labor behavior, demographic demand |
| Technological | Breakthroughs, cost curves, standards, open-source diffusion |
| Legal | Regulation, litigation, IP, compliance |
| Environmental | Energy use, sustainability rules, climate constraints |

Use only the factors that materially affect the topic.

## Porter Five Forces

Use for industry structure:

| Force | Evidence To Collect |
|---|---|
| Rivalry | Number and strength of competitors, price competition, switching costs |
| New entrants | Capital needs, regulation, distribution, talent, patents |
| Substitutes | Alternative workflows, older technologies, manual processes |
| Supplier power | Chips, data, cloud, energy, licenses, key inputs |
| Buyer power | Concentrated customers, procurement cycles, churn, price sensitivity |

Avoid generic ratings. Tie each force to specific companies, data, or events.

## SWOT

Use SWOT only after evidence collection.

| Quadrant | Rule |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Internal advantages with evidence |
| Weaknesses | Internal constraints with evidence |
| Opportunities | External openings tied to timing |
| Threats | External risks tied to actors or rules |

Keep each bullet concrete and source-backed.

## Scenario Analysis

Use three scenarios in the integrated insight section:

- **Base case**: most likely path if current momentum continues.
- **Downside case**: failure mode triggered by regulation, capital freeze, technical ceiling, trust loss, or adoption friction.
- **Upside case**: acceleration path triggered by cost decline, product-market fit, policy support, distribution breakthrough, or ecosystem lock-in.

For each scenario, specify triggers, observable leading indicators, and expected consequences.

## Evidence Quality Ladder

Use this ladder when facts conflict:

1. Official filings, regulator/government pages, court documents, standards documents.
2. Company official announcements, product docs, release notes, investor materials.
3. Peer-reviewed papers, arXiv with author/institution context, benchmark pages.
4. Reputable original journalism with named sources and dates.
5. Public community data, GitHub metrics, app store reviews, forum posts.
6. Aggregators, reposts, unsourced social posts, SEO summaries.

Prefer higher-rung sources for hard facts. Use lower-rung sources for sentiment and discovery signals.
